title: "A personalized daily morning briefing"
description: "Set up a curated start-of-day email — your calendar shape, the meetings worth flagging, and your open items — and tailor it by chatting with Curia."
---

A morning briefing is one short email that tells you what your day looks like before it starts: the shape of the day, only the events worth your attention, and the open items you shouldn't lose track of. It combines a **forward-looking calendar view** with an **open-items digest** (your tasks, pending approvals, and anything Curia has queued for your review) into a single, scannable message.

You set it up on demand and then refine it by talking to Curia — no config files, no redeploys.

## What you'll get

- A one-line read on the day: how many real meetings, when they start and end, whether it's light or back-to-back.
- Only the noteworthy events — with conflicts, travel, and unusual timing flagged — instead of a wall of every calendar block.
- Your overdue and due-today items, open-task counts, and any approvals waiting on you.
- Anything Curia has queued for a quick approve/dismiss (like a proposed update to how it writes on your behalf).

## How it works

The briefing runs as an **on-demand scheduled job** that Curia creates when you ask for it. That matters for one reason: it stays yours to edit. You can change the time or the wording later just by asking, and your changes stick.

<Note>
  This is deliberately **not** a job declared in agent YAML. Declarative schedules are reconciled back to their YAML definition on every restart (see [Recurring jobs in agent YAML](/core-concepts/scheduling#recurring-jobs-in-agent-yaml)), which would quietly revert any personalization. Creating the briefing on demand keeps it editable in plain-language chat.
</Note>

## Set it up

Ask Curia in plain language, naming the time you want it to land:

```
> Set me up a daily morning briefing at 6:30am, America/Toronto.
```

Curia will create the recurring job for you. To shape what it contains, give it an instruction like the one below — adapt any part of it to your preferences:

```
Compile and send the principal's daily morning briefing for today. This one
email combines the day-ahead calendar view with an open-items digest.

1. Day-ahead calendar (use the Principal Contact Details block to resolve
   identity; treat free-status events as available, not commitments):
   - LEAD with the shape of the day in one line: how many real meetings, first
     start, last end, and whether it's light / normal / back-to-back.
   - List ONLY noteworthy events, in time order. For each: time, title, and the
     one thing that matters (who, prep needed, dial-in vs in-person).
   - FLAG explicitly: scheduling conflicts / double-bookings, travel or commute
     legs, anything at an unusual time (very early, late, over lunch, weekend).
   - SKIP the noise: don't list buffers, holds, focus blocks, or declined
     events; don't restate routine recurring holds unless something changed.
   - If there are no noteworthy events, say so in one sentence — don't pad.

2. Open items digest:
   - Call task-list three times: owner "ceo" with no date filter (all open CEO
     tasks); owner "ceo" with due_before end of today (overdue or due today);
     owner "external" with no date filter (waiting-on-others).
   - Call list-pending-actions for open approval requests. If it errors, note
     briefly that approvals couldn't be retrieved and continue.
   - Lead this section with any overdue or due-today CEO tasks — flag them
     prominently — then the open-task counts, then pending approvals.

3. Learning digest:
   - Call list-learning-digest for the proposed writing-voice guide update and
     any sent-mail task-completion undo/confirm items. Include its
     sections_markdown only when non-empty.
   - When the CEO later replies approve / dismiss / undo / confirm for a
     learning item, call resolve-learning-digest with the matching action.

Skip sending entirely if all three task-list calls return empty,
list-pending-actions returns no pending approvals, and list-learning-digest has
no items. Keep it scannable and tight enough to read on a phone. Send via the
principal's preferred channel from the Principal Contact Details block. If the
calendar can't be read, say so plainly rather than inventing a schedule. Sign
off as the CEO's executive assistant.
```

You don't have to get the wording perfect up front. Set it up with whatever's close, then tune it as you see the first few briefings land.

## Adjust it later

Because the job is yours, you change it the same way you made it — by asking. For example:

```
> Move my morning briefing to 7am.
> In the briefing, stop mentioning events I've declined.
> Add my overdue tasks to the top of the briefing.
> Pause my morning briefing while I'm on vacation next week.
```

Curia finds the existing job and edits it in place, so you keep one briefing that evolves — not a pile of duplicates.

<Note>
  The morning briefing subsumes what used to be a separate fixed daily digest. If you want the open-items recap without the calendar view (or vice versa), just tell Curia which sections to keep when you set it up.
</Note>
