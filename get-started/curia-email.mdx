## What this is for

Curia needs an email address of its own, the way an assistant has their own inbox. People write to it, your principal forwards or CCs it, and Curia replies from it, signed as Curia.

This page covers the **Email** step on the [Setup page](/get-started/check-your-setup). It connects **Curia's** mailbox, not your principal's. Giving Curia access to your principal's email and calendar is the **Email & calendar** step: [Your principal's email and calendar](/get-started/principal-email-calendar). That step reuses the Nylas key and Google sign-in you set up here, so most of the work is done once.

Both kinds of mailbox connect the same way: you click **Connect with Google**, sign in as that Google account, and Curia stores the access itself. There is no grant ID to copy. (Pasting a grant ID from the Nylas dashboard still works as a fallback.)

## What you'll need

- **A new mailbox just for Curia,** such as `curia@example.com`, on **Google Workspace or Gmail**. Don't reuse a person's inbox: Curia reads and acts on everything that arrives. Microsoft 365 and other providers aren't supported for sign-in yet.
- **A Nylas account and application.** Curia talks to email through [Nylas](https://nylas.com). Use the application only for Curia: Curia removes Google connections in it that it created but doesn't use. Either kind works:
  - **Sandbox** (free): comes with Nylas's own Google connector, so there's no Google Cloud setup. It allows **5 connected accounts**, enough for Curia's mailbox and a few of your principal's. Nylas positions it for development, so it may change its terms.
  - **Production:** for more than 5 accounts, or to sign in through your own Google app. It needs the Google Cloud steps below.
- **A Google Cloud account,** for a production application only, to create one OAuth client. This is the part that takes longest.
- **An HTTPS address for Curia.** Set `APP_ORIGIN` to Curia's public `https://` address (see [Deployment](/get-started/deployment)). The sign-in callback must be reachable from the browser you sign in with, and sign-in stays unavailable while `APP_ORIGIN` isn't `https://`.
- **The console,** signed in as in [Install Curia](/get-started/installation), open on the Setup page.

**Time:** about 15 minutes with a sandbox application, or about an hour with a production one (most of it is Google Cloud), plus however long it takes to create the mailbox.

**Cost** (checked 2026-11-01):

- Nylas Free: $0 for up to 5 connected accounts. Nylas describes it as "for proving it works" and doesn't say whether it covers production use. Whether the free plan allows a custom Google connector isn't confirmed, so check on your plan.
- Nylas Essentials: $15/month for up to 10 accounts.
- Plus whatever your email provider charges for one more mailbox.

Plan for at least two Nylas accounts: this one, and your principal's mailbox in the Email & calendar step.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create Curia's mailbox">
    Create the mailbox with your email provider. Pick an address you're happy for your principal's contacts to see, because Curia's emails come from it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create a Nylas application and copy its API key">
    Sign up at [nylas.com](https://www.nylas.com) and open the dashboard at [dashboard-v3.nylas.com](https://dashboard-v3.nylas.com). Use the **sandbox** application Nylas creates for you, or create a **production** application (see [What you'll need](#what-youll-need)). Copy its **API key**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Paste the API key in the Email step">
    On the Setup page, open the **Email** step and paste the key into **Nylas API key**. It saves when you leave the field, and then shows **Saved**. It's stored in the vault as `channel.email.nylas_api_key`.

    The **Email & calendar** step shows the same field. It's one key, so it reads **Saved** there too. To change the key after Setup, use **Settings → Channels → email**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Apply and restart, then check the Nylas section">
    Google sign-in works only once Curia has restarted with the key saved. Open **Apply & restart** and click **Apply and restart**.

    Then open the **Email & calendar** step. Its **Nylas** section reads Nylas live using the key you saved. It has three steps, each marked ✓ when done:

    1. **Region.** Curia detects the region your application lives in (US or EU) and saves it as `channel.email.nylas_region`. Use **Change** to set it by hand.
    2. **Callback.** Curia registers its sign-in callback, `https://<your-domain>/api/mailbox-signin/callback`, with your Nylas application. A **Register callback** button appears only if that fails.
    3. **Google connector.** A sandbox application has one built in, so this is already ✓ and the section reads **Ready**: skip to **Connect Curia's mailbox** below. A production application needs the next three steps.

    Until every step is done the section reads **Not ready** and says what's missing. Registering the callback needs no restart. A region other than the one Curia started with (an EU application, say), or a key or region changed later, needs one more: until then the section reads **Restart Curia to finish setting up sign-in.** Use **Apply & restart** again. After that, connecting a mailbox needs no restart of yours.

    After Setup, the same section is under **Settings → Behaviour → Email & Calendar**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Production application only: create the Google Cloud project and consent screen">
    In [Google Cloud](https://console.cloud.google.com), create a project, or use one you already have for Curia. Then open **APIs & Services → OAuth consent screen** and choose the user type. This decision matters:

    | Situation | Choose |
    |---|---|
    | Curia's mailbox and your principal are on **Google Workspace** and you can create the project inside that organisation | **Internal.** No verification and no warning screen. Only accounts in that organisation can sign in. |
    | Anything else, including a mix of Workspace and Gmail accounts | **External**, then set the publishing status to **In production**. It stays unverified. See [Why an unverified app is OK](#why-an-unverified-app-is-ok). |

    <Warning>
      **Never leave an External app in Testing.** In Testing, Google expires refresh tokens after 7 days, so every mailbox would stop working weekly and need reconnecting. Click **Publish app** to move it to **In production**.
    </Warning>

    Add these scopes to the consent screen:

    - `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.modify`
    - `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.compose`
    - `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar`

    Enable the **Gmail API** and **Google Calendar API** for the project, under **APIs & Services → Library**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Production application only: create a Web OAuth client with Nylas's redirect">
    Under **APIs & Services → Credentials**, click **Create credentials → OAuth client ID**. Set the application type to **Web application**. A Desktop client won't work.

    Under **Authorised redirect URIs**, add Nylas's address for your region:

    - US: `https://api.us.nylas.com/v3/connect/callback`
    - EU: `https://api.eu.nylas.com/v3/connect/callback`

    The console shows the exact address for your region under step 3 of the **Nylas** section. Copy it from there. Then click **Create**.

    <Warning>
      Google shows the **client secret only once**, when you create the client. Copy it straight into Curia in the next step. If you miss it, create a new secret.
    </Warning>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Production application only: create the Google connector">
    Back in the **Nylas** section of the **Email & calendar** step, under step 3, paste the **Client ID** and **Client secret** and click **Create connector**. Curia passes the secret to Nylas and doesn't keep it. The fields clear once Nylas has it. The Pub/Sub topic field is optional and only matters for Nylas mail push notifications.

    When the section reads **Ready.**, you can add mailboxes. If it doesn't, the message after **Not ready.** names the next thing to fix, and **Check again** re-reads Nylas:

    | The section says (after "Not ready.") | What to do |
    |---|---|
    | Save the Nylas API key under Channels → Email first. | Paste it in the **Email** step, or under **Settings → Channels → email** after Setup. |
    | Restart Curia to finish setting up sign-in. | The API key or region was saved or changed since Curia started. Use **Apply & restart** (after Setup: **Settings → System → About**). |
    | Nylas rejected the API key. Check the key under Channels → Email, then detect its region. | Check the key, then use **Detect**: a key only works in its own region. |
    | Set APP_ORIGIN to this console's public https:// address and restart Curia. | Set it to Curia's public `https://` address and restart. |
    | Register the sign-in callback with Nylas (step 2). | Click **Register callback** in step 2. |
    | Create the Google connector (step 3). | Fill in the client ID and secret in step 3 (production applications only). |
    | Nylas did not answer. Check again in a moment. | Wait a moment and click **Check again**. |
    | Curia could not read its saved Nylas settings. … | Click **Check again**; if it persists, check the server logs. |
  </Step>

  <Step title="Production application, Workspace accounts: ask the admin to trust the app">
    A Workspace admin can block apps they haven't approved, and a blocked sign-in fails for the person signing in. To avoid that, ask the admin to trust the OAuth client **before** anyone signs in:

    **Admin console → Security → Access and data control → API controls → Manage Third-Party App Access → Add app → OAuth App Name or Client ID**, enter the client ID from the previous step and set it to **Trusted**. It can take up to 24 hours to apply.

    If you skip this and the account is blocked, the sign-in page tells the person to ask their admin to mark Curia's app as Trusted, so it's recoverable. It's just quicker to do first.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect Curia's mailbox">
    Go back to the **Email** step. Under **Curia's mailbox**, click **Connect with Google**. If the button is greyed out, the line under it says what's missing. Google asks you to sign in and approve access. Sign in as **Curia's mailbox**, not your principal's, and leave both the mail and calendar boxes ticked.

    When it's done, the page says **Connected**. Curia adds the account (named `curia` if that name is free) and saves its grant in the vault. **Curia then restarts itself a few seconds later**, and the page says when it's back (about a minute). You don't need to run anything.

    The link the button opens lasts 60 minutes and works once. If it expires or you close the page, click **Connect with Google** again.

    To use a grant ID from the Nylas dashboard instead, open **Paste a grant ID instead**, and enter an account name, Curia's address and the grant ID.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Apply and restart">
    Once the key and a mailbox are both there, the Setup page turns the email channel on by itself; the step reads **Saved. Turns on when you apply and restart at the end.** The channel starts at the next **Apply & restart**, which you can leave until you've finished the other steps. The **Email** step then reads **Done.**

    After Setup, add more mailboxes under **Settings → Channels → email**. Adding one with **Connect with Google** restarts Curia by itself.
  </Step>
</Steps>

For more mailboxes, field details and troubleshooting, see the [email channel reference](/channels/email-setup).

## Why an unverified app is OK

Google classes `gmail.modify` and `gmail.compose` as **restricted** scopes. Fully verifying an app for restricted scopes requires a paid security assessment, which makes no sense for one executive's assistant. You don't need it.

- **Google allows it for small, known groups.** Its restricted-scope verification page says verification isn't needed "if you are the only user of your app or if your app is used by only a few users, all of whom are known personally to you". Those users click through an "unverified app" warning instead ([Google, restricted-scope verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification), read 2026-11-17). Google's policies describe a personal-use app as one used by fewer than 100 people, all known to you ([OAuth policies](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/policies), updated 2026-08-05).
- **The 100-user cap is per project, for the life of the project** ([Google Cloud, Audience](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945), read 2026-11-17). One Curia install uses two or three accounts, so it never gets close.
- **It works in practice.** On 2026-11-17, a Curia production install's Google Drive client was External, In production and unverified, and Google's own Verification Center said its data access was not verified. Its restricted `drive` scope had kept working for more than 7 days. So an unverified production app is neither blocked nor on 7-day tokens.

What can still go wrong, so you aren't surprised:

- **Google can revoke tokens "at any time without notice"** for an app that is no longer approved (same policies page). Nothing on Curia's side can prevent that. If it happens, Curia's health check notices that a mailbox stopped working and alerts by email. For your principal's mailboxes the alert carries a reconnect link that lasts 7 days.
- **A password change on a Google account invalidates its Gmail tokens.** Reconnect the mailbox. Tokens also lapse after 6 months unused.
- **Google deletes OAuth clients inactive for 6 months**, after an email warning ([Google Cloud, OAuth client management](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549257), read 2026-11-17). A live install uses its client daily, but a test install you leave alone might lose it.
- **An Advanced Protection account can't use an unverified External app.** Use an Internal app, or ask whether your admin can trust the client.
- **A Workspace admin can block unapproved apps** for their users. That's what step 8 is for.
- **Nylas's own cookbook says** restricted-scope apps can get `access_denied` for external users until verified, and elsewhere describes the same click-through warning (its "Fix Google access_denied" cookbook, updated 2026-10-07). Our evidence is the production run above. If your first sign-in is hard-blocked, see [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting).

## Check it works

- **The page.** After you connect, the result page says **Connected**, then that Curia is back and using the mailbox.
- **Logs.** After the channel restart, run `docker compose logs curia | grep -i "email channel"`. You should see `Email channel: 1 account(s) configured`. If you see `email channel disabled` instead, the message says what's missing.
- **Health.** `curl https://<your-domain>/api/health` shows `"email": "ok"`. `"skipped"` means the email channel didn't start. `"fail"` means it started but can't reach the mailbox: reconnect it.
- **Console.** After **Apply & restart**, the Setup page's **Email** step reads **Done.** After Setup, the email row in **Settings → Channels** shows **enabled**.
- **A real message.** Send a short email to Curia's address from your own mailbox. Curia checks every 30 seconds, so give it a minute.

## Troubleshooting

When a sign-in doesn't finish, the page it ends on says why:

| The page says | What happened and what to do |
|---|---|
| Link expired | The link ran out. Links last 60 minutes. Click **Connect with Google** for a new one. |
| Link not valid | The link isn't one Curia recognises. Click **Connect with Google** for a new one. |
| Link already used | Links work once. If the mailbox isn't connected, click **Connect with Google** for a new one. |
| Sign-in failed | Something went wrong while connecting. Try again, and if it keeps failing, ask for help. |
| Something went wrong | Curia couldn't tell what happened. Get a new link and try again. |
| Sign-in cancelled | The Google sign-in was closed or cancelled. The link is now used: click **Connect with Google** for a new one. |
| Blocked by your Google admin | The Workspace admin blocks unapproved apps. Ask them to mark the client ID **Trusted** (step 8), then try again. |
| Account not allowed | The Google app is **Internal** to one organisation, and the account is outside it. Switch the app's audience to External, then get a fresh sign-in link (the old one is used). |
| Wrong account | You signed in with a different Google account than the one requested. The link is now used: get a new one and pick the right account. |
| Permissions missing | Google asks for mail and calendar access, sometimes on two screens. Leave **every** box ticked, then try again. |
| Not a Google account | Only Google accounts connect this way. |
| Not connected | Curia couldn't accept this mailbox. Check the address and the console settings. |
| Account limit reached | Nylas's account limit is reached. A sandbox allows 5 connected accounts. Remove an unused mailbox, or move to a production application. |
| Reconnected | The mailbox was already set up. Its access was refreshed with this new sign-in. Nothing else to do. |

A hard "This app is blocked" screen from Google, or `access_denied` even after you accept the unverified-app warning, means Google is applying a stricter policy to your project than described above. Try an **Internal** app inside a Workspace organisation instead.
