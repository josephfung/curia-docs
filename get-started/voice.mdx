## What this is for

Voice lets your principal talk to Curia out loud, in a live two-way call, instead of typing. Curia listens, works the same way it does in chat, and answers in a spoken voice that you choose here.

This page covers the **Voice** step on the [Setup page](/get-started/check-your-setup). Voice is a required step, so your principal can choose a call or chat for any session. For ports, settings and troubleshooting, see the [Voice reference](/channels/voice-setup).

## What you'll need

- **A Deepgram account** for speech-to-text (turning speech into words).
- **A Cartesia account** for text-to-speech (Curia's spoken voice).
- **LiveKit,** the server that carries call audio. It comes with Curia: the install's `docker-compose.yml` includes a `livekit` service. The Setup page doesn't ask for LiveKit's keys, but an install that doesn't have them yet needs the extra step below.
- **HTTPS for the console** on any host other than `localhost`. Browsers only allow the microphone on a secure page.
- **For a server install:** an HTTPS route to LiveKit's signaling port, and two ports open to the internet for call audio (`7881/tcp` and `7882/udp`). See [Run LiveKit on a server](/channels/voice-setup#run-livekit-on-a-server).
- **For Signal calls only:** the Signal channel, and server-side pieces the standard install doesn't include. See [Signal calls](/channels/voice-setup#signal-calls).

**Time:** about 20 minutes to try it on a laptop. Allow an hour on a server, mostly for LiveKit's config and firewall.

**Cost** (checked 2026-11-01):

- LiveKit runs on your own server, with no separate fee.
- Deepgram: $200 of free credit, then Nova-3 streaming at $0.0077 per minute.
- Cartesia: a free tier of 20,000 credits (one credit per character spoken), which appears not to include a commercial licence. Pro is $5/month for 100,000 credits.

<Warning>
  Call audio and transcripts leave your server. They go to Deepgram, Cartesia and your LLM provider. Read their data terms before you turn voice on.
</Warning>

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose how your principal will call">
    On the Setup page, open the **Voice** step and pick one. The choice saves as soon as you pick it.

    - **In the Curia app:** your principal taps to call from the phone app or the console chat, in a web browser or in the [phone app](/channels/phone-app).
    - **Signal call:** your principal calls Curia's Signal number. Experimental: see [Signal calls](/channels/voice-setup#signal-calls).
    - **Phone:** a dedicated phone line. Shown as coming soon; it can't be picked yet.

    The step marks the choice that matches your Messaging route.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Get a Deepgram API key">
    Sign up at [deepgram.com](https://deepgram.com) and create an API key.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Get a Cartesia API key and choose Curia's voice">
    Sign up at [cartesia.ai](https://cartesia.ai) and create an API key.

    Then choose the voice your principal will hear. Browse and preview voices at [play.cartesia.ai](https://play.cartesia.ai), and copy the **voice ID** (a UUID) of the one you want. It's worth choosing with your principal in mind: they'll hear this voice on every call.

    The voice ID is required. Voice stays off until it's set.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Enter them in the Voice step">
    Fill in the three fields and click **Save** (or press Enter). Each shows **Saved** once it's in the [vault](/get-started/secrets-vault):

    | Field | What to enter |
    |---|---|
    | Deepgram API key | From Deepgram |
    | Cartesia API key | From Cartesia |
    | Curia's voice | The Cartesia voice ID |

    Once these and LiveKit's keys are in, the voice channel turns on by itself and starts at **Apply & restart**. To change the keys or the voice after Setup, use **Settings → Channels → Voice**, then restart.
  </Step>

  <Step title="If the step asks for LiveKit's keys: start LiveKit and add them">
    If the step says **Voice also needs LiveKit's keys, which come with Curia's install; this install doesn't have them yet**, start LiveKit and enter its three values yourself. Run these commands from the directory you installed Curia into.

    **To try voice on the machine you're sitting at** (`http://localhost:3000`), start LiveKit in development mode:

    ```bash
    docker compose --profile voice up -d livekit
    ```

    Development mode uses the well-known key `devkey` and secret `secret`, and its signaling URL is `ws://localhost:7880`.

    **On a server,** don't use development mode: anyone who can reach the ports could use those well-known credentials. Give LiveKit its own key and secret, put HTTPS in front of its signaling port, and open the media ports. Follow [Run LiveKit on a server](/channels/voice-setup#run-livekit-on-a-server).

    Then open **Settings → Channels → Voice** (the Settings pages stay open during Setup) and fill in:

    | Field | What to enter |
    |---|---|
    | LiveKit signaling URL | `ws://localhost:7880` for the laptop try-out, or `wss://<your-domain>` on a server |
    | LiveKit API key | `devkey`, or your own key on a server |
    | LiveKit API secret | `secret`, or your own secret on a server |

    Voice has no `.env` fallback: these live in the vault too. Then go back to the **Voice** step and click **Save and continue**; the channel turns on once every key is there.
  </Step>

  <Step title="For a Signal call: add Signal too">
    A Signal call needs the Signal channel. If your Messaging route is Signal, it's already set up. Otherwise the **Voice** step asks for **Curia's Signal number**, the **Signal server socket** and **your principal's Signal number**, as in [Reach your principal](/get-started/reach-your-principal).

    Signal calls also need `SIGNAL_VOICE_CALLS_ENABLED=true` and `SIGNAL_PULSE_SOCKET_PATH` in the server's `.env`, plus a newer signal-cli, a call tunnel and PulseAudio. The step's status line names what's missing. See [Signal calls](/channels/voice-setup#signal-calls).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Apply and restart">
    Click **Save and continue**. Voice starts at the next **Apply & restart**, which you can leave until you've finished the other steps. The **Voice** step then reads **Done.**
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Check it works

1. Open `/api/health` on your Curia address. `checks.voice` should read `ok`. It reads `fail` if Curia can't reach LiveKit, and `skipped` if voice didn't start.
2. Open the console **Chat**. A **Voice** button (with a phone icon) appears once voice is running. If it's missing, see [The Voice button doesn't appear](/channels/voice-setup#troubleshooting).
3. Click **Voice**, allow the microphone, and say something. Curia greets you first, then answers out loud. Hang up from the call bar. The transcript appears in the chat history. If the console shows **Curia couldn't join the call**, see [Troubleshooting](/channels/voice-setup#troubleshooting).

The **Voice** step reading **Done.** means the voice channel is running. The test call is the real check. Console chat opens after Start onboarding.

## What your principal will notice

- A **Voice** button in the console chat. They click it, allow the microphone, and talk.
- Curia speaks in the voice you chose. It greets them first, waits a moment when they pause mid-sentence, and stops talking as soon as they interrupt.
- For longer jobs, Curia says it will get back to them rather than leaving silence on the line.
- Each call's transcript is saved to the chat history, so they can scroll back to it.
- They can have Curia prepare an agenda for their next call, or ask it to take notes on any call. Afterwards Curia writes minutes and sends one follow-up. See [Agendas, notes and minutes](/channels/voice-setup#agendas-notes-and-minutes).
- In the phone app, locking the phone or switching apps pauses the call instead of dropping it.

Whoever uses the console chat is treated as your principal, so a test call you make is a conversation as them, and an instruction on the call counts as their approval. Keep test calls short and neutral.
