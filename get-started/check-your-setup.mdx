## What this is for

On a new install, the console opens on the **Setup page** (`/setup`), full screen. It lists every step, in order, and takes every key and detail on the step itself, so you never have to leave it. When the required steps pass, **Start onboarding** ends Setup and hands Curia over to your principal.

This page describes the Setup page as a whole. The steps that need outside accounts each have their own page in this section, and each step on the Setup page links to its page with a **Setup guide** link.

An install that was set up before the Setup page existed never sees it.

## What you'll need

- The console address and your access key (the bootstrap secret the installer printed). See [Install Curia](/get-started/installation).
- The details and keys each step asks for. Each step's page lists them.
- About 1–2 hours in all, mostly for the email, messaging and voice accounts. You can stop at any point: the Setup page opens again where you left off.

## How the page works

- **The step list.** On the left, every step with its mark: done (✓), waiting for a restart, not done, or locked. The top shows how many of the seven required steps are done. Click any step to open it. When you come back, the page opens on the first required step that isn't done.
- **One button per step.** Each step has one **Save and continue** button, which saves anything still typed and moves to the next step. Each group of key and number fields also has its own **Save** button (Enter works too); nothing saves just because you leave a field. A saved field shows **Saved**, and typing in it again replaces the value. Choices, such as a messaging route or a tone, save as soon as you pick them.
- **The status line.** Each step shows its live check as one line: **Done.**, what's still missing, or **Saved. Turns on when you apply and restart at the end.**
- **Channels turn on by themselves.** A channel (email, Signal, text message, Slack, voice) turns on as soon as its keys are in. It starts at **Apply & restart**.
- **Phone numbers** are saved in international (E.164) format, such as `+14165550100`. Spaces, dashes and brackets you type are dropped; a number without its country code is refused.
- **The rest of the console waits.** Until Start onboarding, the home page and chat open the Setup page instead. The Settings pages stay open, for anything a step doesn't cover.

## The steps

| 1 | **Who it's for** | Yes | Myself or someone else; your name and email as the operator (someone else only); the principal's name, title, email, timezone and pronouns | [Below](#who-its-for) |
| 2 | **Curia's name** | Yes | Curia's name, title and email signature | [Below](#curias-name) |
| 3 | **Email** | Yes | The Nylas API key, then Curia's own mailbox | [Curia's own email](/get-started/curia-email) |
| 4 | **Messaging** | Yes | How Curia messages the principal: the Curia app, Signal, text message or Slack, with that route's keys and the principal's number or ID on it | [Reach your principal](/get-started/reach-your-principal) |
| 5 | **Voice** | Yes | How the principal talks to Curia: the Curia app or a Signal call, with the Deepgram and Cartesia keys and Curia's voice | [Voice](/get-started/voice) |
| 6 | **Email & calendar** | Yes | The Nylas sign-in checks, so the principal can connect their Google accounts | [Your principal's email and calendar](/get-started/principal-email-calendar) |

| 8 | **Apply & restart** | | Nothing: one restart turns on everything you saved. The channel steps pass only after it | [Below](#apply-and-restart) |
| 9 | **Start onboarding** | | Locked until steps 1–7 pass. Ends Setup | [Below](#start-onboarding) |

Steps 6 and 7 are grouped as **Business Apps**: the business applications Curia is authorized to use.

After Start onboarding, the step list shows three **Optional extras**. They never block Start onboarding:

| Extra | What it asks for | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Working style** | A starting tone and how independently Curia acts | [Below](#working-style) |
| **Higher search limit** | A Tavily API key | [Tavily web search](/integrations/tavily) |
| **Capabilities** | Switches for what Curia does | [Below](#capabilities) |

The setup guide checks two more optional items that aren't steps on the page. Ask it "what's left to set up?" to see them:

| Item | What it is | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Principal mailboxes and calendars** | Your principal's own Google accounts, connected with **Connect with Google**. Your principal can also connect them in their first conversation | [Your principal's email and calendar](/get-started/principal-email-calendar) |
| **Web browser** | The `browser` container, for pages that need JavaScript, sign-ins or forms. It needs no key | [Web browser](/integrations/web-browser) |

<Tip>
  **Google sign-in needs a restart first.** **Connect with Google** works only once Curia has restarted with the Nylas API key saved. On a new install: paste the key in **Email**, then click **Apply & restart**. Open **Email & calendar**, which detects your Nylas region and registers Curia's sign-in callback by itself. Then go back to **Email** and connect Curia's mailbox. Curia restarts itself after that sign-in; apply and restart once more at the end to start the email channel. Pasting a grant ID instead of signing in skips the first restart.
</Tip>

### Who it's for

Choose **Myself** or **Someone else**.

- **Myself:** you set Curia up, then use it yourself as the principal. Console chat starts a fresh conversation at Start onboarding.
- **Someone else:** you set Curia up for an executive you work for. Add **your** name and email first. Curia treats you as its operator, the person who looks after setup and admin, and later brings admin and support questions to you rather than to the principal.

Then add the principal's details: full name, email and timezone (required), and title and pronouns (optional). The timezone starts as your browser's. If you're setting Curia up for someone else, the principal can correct any of these later.

Click **Save and continue**.

### Curia's name

The name, title and email signature Curia uses in the emails and messages it sends. On a new install, the page suggests a first name and a title such as "Executive Assistant to Dana Okafor". The signature is optional.

Click **Save and continue**. You can change these later under **Settings → Behaviour → Personality**.

### Apply and restart

Curia restarts once to turn on everything you saved, then checks every step again. It takes about a minute, and the page reconnects by itself. Before you click, the step lists what's waiting for a restart.

Click **Apply and restart**, then **Continue** once it says **Restarted**. You can come back and apply again after more changes.

- **"Too many restarts in a row."** Wait a few minutes and try again.
- **"Curia hasn't come back yet."** Check that the container restarted (`docker compose ps`), then reload the page.

### Start onboarding

Locked until steps 1–7 pass. While it's locked, the step says which steps are left. It has two buttons, and both end Setup:

- **Start onboarding** ends Setup and starts your principal's onboarding.
- **Not right now** ends Setup without starting it. It asks you to confirm first: onboarding is required before Curia acts for your principal, and Curia won't contact them until it starts. The console's home page then shows **Onboarding hasn't started.** with a **Start onboarding** button, until it runs.

When you click **Start onboarding**, Curia:

1. **Switches on Email triage, but holds it.** Curia doesn't touch your principal's inbox until it has told them what to expect, in their first conversation. If this needs a restart, the step waits for Curia to come back, as **Apply & restart** does.
2. **Does its homework.** It looks up your principal's public professional footprint (company, role, public bio, recent news; never family, home or personal accounts) so it can open with what it found and ask what it got wrong. Nothing from it is remembered until your principal confirms it.
3. **Starts a fresh console chat,** so your principal never sees the Setup chat.
4. **Reaches your principal** on the messaging route you chose:
   - **The Curia app:** the step shows an **invitation link**, with a message to send your principal (or, if Curia is for you, a link to open on your phone). The link installs Curia on their phone and signs them in. It works once and lasts 48 hours, and Curia says hello once they're in. **The link is shown only once:** copy it before you leave the page. If you lose it, create a new one under **Settings → System → Devices**.
   - **Signal, text message or Slack:** the step says **Curia has said hello**. Curia sends the greeting itself.

If the step can't start onboarding, it says why:

- **Curia already looks in use.** Email triage is already on, or your principal already has tasks or conversations with Curia. The step asks you to confirm, then starts.
- **Curia can't reach your principal yet.** Check that the channel is on in **Settings → Channels**, and that your principal's number or ID is on their contact.
- **Memory is off.** Onboarding keeps its progress in Curia's memory, so it needs it on. See [Check it all works](#check-it-all-works).
- **Onboarding has already started.** There's nothing more to do here.

Then click **Open the console**. The console leaves the Setup page and loads its home page.

Before you click Start onboarding, read [Check it all works](#check-it-all-works) and [Handing over to your principal](#handing-over-to-your-principal).

## Optional extras

Each extra is listed after Start onboarding, without a number. **Save and continue** walks them in order and returns to Start onboarding. Do them before you click Start onboarding: the Setup page closes after it. Each extra's settings are also under **Settings**, for later.

### Working style

A starting point for how Curia sounds and how much it does on its own. If you leave them, the defaults below apply until the principal changes them. Each choice saves as soon as you pick it.

- **Tone:** Dry & understated, Warm & personable (the default), Crisp & direct, or Formal.
- **Independence:**
  - **Draft only:** Curia prepares drafts and summaries; the principal sends, schedules and commits.
  - **Ask first** (the default): Curia carries out the principal's instructions, and asks before acting on its own initiative.
  - **Spot-check:** Curia goes ahead with most things and flags a few for the principal to review.

Later, change them under **Settings → Behaviour → Personality** and **Settings → Behaviour → Autonomy**. See [Autonomy](/core-concepts/autonomy).

### Higher search limit

Web search works without a key, at a few dozen searches an hour. A free Tavily key raises the limit. See [Tavily web search](/integrations/tavily).

### Capabilities

Switches for what Curia does for the principal. Most people keep the defaults.

| Capability | What it does | On a new install |
|---|---|---|
| **Meetings** | Agendas before a meeting, notes during it, minutes after. The principal's onboarding sessions run on it. | Always on: no switch |
| **Meeting debriefs** | After a meeting, Curia asks the principal a few quick questions and files the follow-ups. | On |
| **Web research** | Searches and reads the web, and hands bigger questions to a research analyst who writes up the findings. | On |
| **Projects and tasks** | Keeps track of work the principal hands off, and follows up until it's done. | On |
| **Diagnostics** | The principal can ask Curia why something didn't work, and get a plain answer. It reads Curia's audit log and working records, and uses Curia's most capable model, which costs more. | Off: turn it on if you want it |
| **Email triage** | Sorts and labels the principal's inbox, and archives what doesn't need attention. | Off. On the Setup page it's locked: it acts on the principal's own inbox, so it's left for them to choose later in Settings. |

- **A change applies at the next restart.** During Setup, that's **Apply & restart**.
- **Some capabilities depend on others.** Meeting debriefs and Email triage file their follow-ups with Projects and tasks, so turning either on asks to turn Projects and tasks on too. If Projects and tasks isn't available on the install, the switch says so and changes nothing.
- **Turning one off can affect other parts of Curia.** The page names them and asks first. Web research and Projects and tasks always ask, because Curia's main agent uses them.
- **Other states.** **Partly on** means some of a capability's parts are on: switching it sets them all. **Not available** means a part is missing from this install or broken; the line names it.

After Setup, the same switches are under **Settings → Behaviour → Capabilities**, where Email triage can be switched too. Each capability is a group of agents and tools; the **Agents** and **Tools** pages show them one by one. See [Registry](/core-concepts/registry).

## The setup guide

The setup guide is Curia's own chat, docked on the right of the Setup page. Ask it to explain a step, compare options ("Signal or text message?") or work out why a check isn't passing. It reads the same live checks as the step list, and it knows which step you have open.

- **Opening it.** It starts closed: the steps explain themselves. Click **Need help? Ask the setup guide** at the bottom of any step. It opens with "I'm on the Email step." (or whichever step) in the message box, ready for you to finish and send. The first time, it greets you before you've asked anything.
- **It follows you.** When you open another step, a **Now on:** line marks the change in the conversation.
- **It doesn't fill in steps.** It explains and checks; you enter the keys and details on the steps.
- **Kept out of Curia's memory.** Curia doesn't learn facts about the principal from the Setup chat, and the principal's chat starts fresh at Start onboarding.
- **Smaller screens.** On a narrow window, the step list hides while the guide is open; click **Steps** to bring it back. On a phone, the guide fills the screen.

If the guide says its messages may reach Curia's usual chat, Setup couldn't start properly, or the install was set up outside the Setup page. Then your messages go to Curia's main chat instead.

After Setup, you can still ask Curia for help with something it hasn't set up: "Help me set up voice" reaches the setup guide through Curia's main chat.

## Check it all works

The marks on the Setup page are live checks: a channel's step reads **Done.** only once that channel is running. Two further checks need nothing but a terminal. The end-to-end tests that follow happen in console chat, which opens after Start onboarding.

<Warning>
  After Start onboarding, the console treats everyone in console chat as the principal. Some tests below message the principal's real phone and inbox. If you're setting Curia up for someone else, run them together with your principal, or skip them and use the terminal checks only.
</Warning>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Curia is up and healthy">
    Call the health endpoint. It needs no login:

    ```bash
    curl https://your-curia-domain.example.com/api/health
    ```

    On a fully set-up instance the response looks like this:

    ```json
    {
      "status": "ok",
      "uptime_s": 412,
      "checks": {
        "db": "ok",
        "bus": "ok",
        "signal": "ok",
        "email": "ok",
        "browser": "ok",
        "mcp": { "google_workspace": "ok" },
        "nylas_calendar": "ok",
        "slack": "skipped",
        "sms": "skipped",
        "voice": "ok",
        "signal_voice": "skipped",
        "scheduler": "ok",
        "principal_mailbox_triage": "ok"
      }
    }
    ```

    Check that:

    - `status` is `ok`. `degraded` means one of the checks reads `fail`. `down` (HTTP 503) means the database or the internal message bus is unreachable.
    - `db`, `bus` and `scheduler` are `ok`.
    - `email` and `voice` are `ok`, and so is the field for the messaging route you chose: `signal`, `sms` or `slack`.

    `skipped` means that service isn't configured. That's fine for a route you didn't choose. See [Health monitoring](/get-started/health-monitoring) for what each check probes.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Memory is on">
    In `/api/health`, `knowledge_graph` reads `ok`. If it reads `fail`, memory is off, and so is most of the setup guide; `details.knowledge_graph` names the reason. The startup logs say the same in words:

    ```bash
    docker compose logs curia | grep -i "entity memory"
    ```

    You want `Entity memory initialized with knowledge graph`. A line ending in `knowledge graph unavailable` says what's wrong and how to fix it. Details: [Embedding models](/integrations/embeddings-endpoint#see-which-model-is-active).
  </Step>

  <Step title="The web browser works">
    In `/api/health`, `browser` reads `ok`. On the server, `docker compose ps browser` should say `healthy`. Details: [Web browser](/integrations/web-browser).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Curia's own email sends and receives">
    In console chat, ask Curia: "Send me a test email." It arrives in the principal's inbox, from Curia's own address. Reply to it from the principal's address. Curia answers in the same thread. Details: [Curia's own email](/get-started/curia-email).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Curia can reach your principal">
    In console chat, ask Curia: "Send me a test message on Signal" (or "by text message", or "on Slack"). It should arrive on the principal's phone from Curia. Reply from that phone, and Curia answers.

    If you chose the Curia app, install it on the principal's phone first; see [Use Curia on your phone](/channels/phone-app). Details: [Reach your principal](/get-started/reach-your-principal).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Voice calls work">
    Open console chat. A **Voice** button appears above the message box when voice is available. Click it and allow the microphone. The bar shows "Greeting…" while Curia says hello, then "Listening…". Say something, hear the answer, then click **Hang up**.

    No **Voice** button means voice isn't available. Details: [Voice](/get-started/voice).
  </Step>

  <Step title="The principal's calendar is connected">
    Once the principal has connected a mailbox, `nylas_calendar` in `/api/health` reads `ok`. `skipped` means no principal mailbox is connected yet. In console chat, ask "What's on my calendar tomorrow?" and check the answer. Details: [Your principal's email and calendar](/get-started/principal-email-calendar).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Google Docs works">
    In `/api/health`, `mcp.google_workspace` reads `ok`. Then, in console chat, ask Curia: "List the five newest files in your Google Drive." A list (even an empty one) means it works. Details: [Google Docs](/get-started/google-docs).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Optional: web search">
    Ask Curia in console chat to search the web for something recent. If you added a Tavily key, a daily check also tests it; look for a `Health canary result` log line with `"canary":"tavily"` and `"status":"ok"`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Handing over to your principal

**Start onboarding** is the hand-over: Curia invites your principal to a first conversation and takes it from there. If you set Curia up for yourself, open the invitation on your phone (or answer Curia's hello) and you're done.

If you set it up for someone else:

- **Send the invitation,** if you chose the Curia app. Copy the message from the Start onboarding step and send it to your principal in a text or a message. It works once and lasts 48 hours.
- **Tell your principal to expect Curia,** and roughly what happens next. [Your first conversation with Curia](/onboarding/first-conversation) is a short page you can send them.
- **Give them Curia's email address,** so they can forward mail and write to Curia, and Curia's Signal or text-message number to save as a contact, if you chose one of those.
- **Leave the rest to Curia.** It asks your principal to connect their email and calendar during the first conversation, and explains what that means. [Connecting your email](/onboarding/connecting-your-email) covers it for them.
- **Where to save passwords and cards:** they can hand Curia a website password or a payment card through the one-time link it sends, or add one under **Memory → Secrets**. See [Secrets vault](/get-started/secrets-vault).

### When Curia emails you

If you entered your own name and email in **Who it's for**, Curia emails you, not your principal, when onboarding needs a hand:

| You'll hear when | What to do |
|---|---|
| Your principal hasn't answered the greeting after two follow-ups, 48 hours apart | Check in with them, or check that the route still works |
| The invitation link expired unused | Create a new one under **Settings → System → Devices**, and send it |
| Your principal's Google sign-in was blocked by their Workspace admin | Ask the admin to mark the Google client ID in the email **Trusted** |
| Curia's web search failed during its homework | Nothing urgent: Curia starts with open questions instead. Check [Tavily web search](/integrations/tavily) |
| Curia couldn't finish reading your principal's mailbox, or couldn't deliver its drafts | Check `/api/health`. Curia carries on, and the review uses what it has |

These emails never include your principal's messages, addresses or plans. If you didn't enter an operator, they go to your principal. Change the operator later under **Settings → System → Operator**.

### After hand-over, use Settings, not chat

The console treats everyone in console chat as the principal. Anything typed there after Start onboarding, including by you, is taken as the principal speaking, and Curia may remember it as their preference.

After hand-over, make changes through the console's pages instead: **Settings → Channels**, **Settings → Behaviour** (including **Capabilities**), **Settings → MCP Skills**, **Settings → System**, **Memory → Contacts** and **Memory → Secrets**.
