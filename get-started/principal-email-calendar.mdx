## What this is for

Much of an assistant's work happens in the executive's own inbox and calendar. Delegated access lets Curia work there as your principal: triage their unread mail, draft replies for them to send, and read and update their calendar. Without it, Curia has no calendar at all, and its inbox triage is off.

This is your **principal's** mailbox, not Curia's. Curia's own address is set up in [2. Curia's own email](/get-started/curia-email). Don't add the principal's mailbox there: email accounts in that list are mailboxes Curia owns and answers from.

Only **Google** accounts (Gmail or Google Workspace) are supported for the principal today. The inbox tools use Gmail's search syntax and labels.

## What you'll need

- **Curia's own email working,** from page 2. This step uses the same Nylas application.
- **Your principal, or their Google sign-in.** Connecting the mailbox means signing in to their Google account, so do it with them.

**Time:** about 20 minutes, plus finding a moment with your principal.

**Cost** (checked 2026-11-01): the principal's mailbox is one more connected account on your Nylas plan. Nylas Free covers up to 5 accounts (Nylas describes it as "for proving it works" and doesn't say whether it covers production use). Essentials is $15/month for up to 10.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect your principal's Google account to Nylas">
    In the Nylas dashboard, open the same application you used for Curia's mailbox. Go to **Grants** and add a grant, the same way you connected Curia's mailbox, but sign in as your **principal's** Google account. Allow access to both mail and calendar.

    Copy the new **grant ID**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save the grant in the console">
    In the console, go to **Settings → Channels**, click the **email** row, and scroll to **Principal's mailbox**. Enter each value and click **Save**:

    | Field | Value | Stored in the vault as |
    |---|---|---|
    | **Nylas grant ID** | The grant ID from the previous step: your principal's mailbox | `ceo_nylas_grant_id` |
    | **Mailbox address** | Your principal's email address | `ceo_self_email` |

    Each row turns to **configured** when it's saved. The inbox tools use the Nylas API key you entered for Curia's email, and skip messages from Curia's own email accounts, so there's nothing else to enter.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn on the inbox and calendar specialists">
    These are off on a new install. In the console:

    - On **Agents**, install and enable `calendar` and `ceo-inbox`.
    - On **Tools**, install and enable the `ceo-inbox` bundle, plus `voice-learn`, `task-completion-from-sent`, `list-learning-digest` and `resolve-learning-digest`. These four let Curia learn from what your principal actually sends.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Restart Curia">
    Curia reads the grant, and the agent and tool changes, only when it starts. Restart from the console's **Settings → System → About** page, or run:

    ```bash
    docker compose restart curia
    ```
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **Coming soon:** your principal will connect their own mailbox by signing in from a link Curia sends, with no grant ID to copy and no restart.
</Note>

## Check it works

1. In the logs (`docker compose logs curia`), look for `Nylas calendar client initialized (bound to the principal's Nylas grant)`. If you see `Calendar disabled — ceo_nylas_grant_id is not configured`, the grant isn't in the vault.
2. Open `/api/health` on your Curia address. `checks.nylas_calendar` should read `ok`. It reads `skipped` when there's no grant.
3. In the console chat, ask "What's on my calendar tomorrow?" and check the answer against the real calendar.

Console chat counts as your principal, so keep test questions to reading, not changing.

## What your principal will notice

**In their inbox**, every hour from 6am to 11pm:

- Curia works through unread mail. Messages it has dealt with are marked read or archived.
- Replies appear as **drafts** in their Drafts folder for them to review and send.
- Some messages get labels, such as `⏳ In Progress` while Curia waits on something, or `⚠️ Stuck` when it needs them.
- Anything urgent reaches them on another channel, such as Signal.

**On their calendar:**

- Curia answers scheduling questions from their real calendar.
- While it negotiates a time, it may place tentative events titled `HOLD (TBC): …`. It clears stale holds every night.
- It can accept invitations and create events as them. At the default autonomy level (75), creating an event waits for their approval first.

Changes appear as made by their own account, because Curia acts as them.
