## What this is for

Much of an assistant's work happens in the executive's own inbox and calendar. Delegated access lets Curia work there as your principal: triage their unread mail, draft replies for them to send, and read and update their calendar. Without it, Curia has no calendar at all, and its inbox triage is off.

Your principal can connect **more than one account**, for example a personal Gmail and a work Google Workspace account. Curia then triages both inboxes, drafts and sends from the right account, puts meetings on the right calendar, and checks availability across every calendar.

These are your **principal's** mailboxes, not Curia's. Curia's own address is set up in the **Email** step: [Curia's own email](/get-started/curia-email). Don't add the principal's mailboxes there: email accounts in that list are mailboxes Curia owns and answers from.

On the [Setup page](/get-started/check-your-setup), the **Email & calendar** step gets Google sign-in ready for your principal's accounts: the Nylas region, Curia's sign-in callback and, for a production Nylas application, the Google connector. That's all the step needs to pass. Connecting a mailbox isn't required to finish Setup: you can connect your principal's accounts with them during Setup, or they can connect them in their first conversation.

Only **Google** accounts (Gmail or Google Workspace) are supported for the principal today. The inbox tools use Gmail's search syntax and labels.

## What you'll need

- **Mailbox sign-in set up,** as in [Curia's own email](/get-started/curia-email): the Nylas API key, region, callback and Google connector, and the **Nylas** section reading **Ready**. Saving the key the first time needs one restart (**Apply & restart**) before it reads **Ready**; connecting mailboxes after that needs none. This step uses the same Nylas application and Google client, so there is no new Google Cloud work.
- **Your principal, or their Google sign-in,** for each account. Connecting a mailbox means signing in to that Google account, so do it with them, or send them the link.

**Time:** about 5 minutes per account, plus finding a moment with your principal.

**Cost** (checked 2026-11-01): each principal mailbox is one more connected account on your Nylas plan. Nylas Free covers up to 5 accounts (Nylas describes it as "for proving it works" and doesn't say whether it covers production use). Essentials is $15/month for up to 10.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Get Google sign-in ready">
    On the Setup page, open the **Email & calendar** step. It shows the same **Nylas API key** field as the **Email** step (one key, so it reads **Saved** if you entered it there), then the **Nylas** section. On its first check the section detects your Nylas region and registers Curia's sign-in callback by itself. A sandbox application then reads **Ready**. A production application also needs its Google connector: see [Curia's own email](/get-started/curia-email), steps 5 to 7.

    If the section reads **Restart Curia to finish setting up sign-in.**, use **Apply & restart**, then come back. The step reads **Done.** once sign-in is ready. After Setup, the same section is at the top of **Settings → Behaviour → Email & Calendar**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check the Google side is ready for this account">
    Two things from [Curia's own email](/get-started/curia-email) matter here:

    - If your principal's account is on **Google Workspace** and the Google app is **External**, ask their admin to mark the OAuth client ID **Trusted** first (the admin step in Curia's own email, step 8). Otherwise a blocked sign-in just tells them to ask their admin, which wastes a moment.
    - Your principal will see Google's **unverified app** screen. This is expected: see [Why an unverified app is OK](/get-started/curia-email#why-an-unverified-app-is-ok). Tell them to open **Advanced** and choose to continue to the app. [Connecting your email](/onboarding/connecting-your-email) is a short page you can send them that explains it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect each of your principal's Google accounts">
    Once sign-in is ready, the **Email & calendar** step shows a **Connect with Google** button. Use it with your principal beside you, or leave it for them to do in their first conversation.

    To see and manage the list of mailboxes, use **Settings → Behaviour → Email & Calendar**. It's open during Setup too. This page is only for your principal's own mail and calendars; Curia's own email accounts stay under **Settings → Channels → Email**.

    If the page shows **Finish setting up the email channel first**, the Nylas API key isn't saved yet. Paste it in the Setup page's **Email** step (or, after Setup, under **Settings → Channels → Email**) and come back. Until then, the page lists any mailboxes already connected but can't add or change them. If the **Connect with Google** button is greyed out, the line under it says what's missing.

    Click **Connect with Google** and sign in as your **principal's** Google account. Google asks for access to mail and calendar, sometimes on two separate screens. **Leave every box ticked on both.** If any is unticked, the sign-in finishes with **Permissions missing** and nothing is connected.

    When it's done, the page says **Connected**. Curia reads the address from the sign-in itself, adds the mailbox to the list with a short id and a label, adds the address to your principal's contact record, and connects its calendar. Signing in as an address that's already in the list reconnects that mailbox instead. There's no address, id or grant ID to type, and **no restart**. Each mailbox in the list also has its own **Reconnect with Google** button, which only accepts that mailbox's account.

    You can also add the account in other ways:

    - **Let your principal connect it.** Your principal can ask Curia to connect or reconnect their Gmail, and Curia replies with a sign-in link for one of their verified email addresses. They can also ask to connect a new address of theirs, for example "connect my new work email", as long as they ask from a signed-in conversation (the console chat, not an email or text from outside). Signing in adds that address to their contact record. A link lasts 60 minutes and works once, and must be used with the account it was made for.
    - **Paste a grant ID.** Under **Paste a grant ID instead**, enter a **Mailbox id** (a short permanent name such as `work`; lowercase letters, digits and hyphens; it can't be changed or reused), a **Label** and the **Grant ID** from the Nylas dashboard. Click **Test** to check the address first, then **Add mailbox**. This is the older route and still works.

    Curia refuses a grant that isn't a Google account, that's already connected, or that belongs to one of Curia's own email accounts. The grant is stored in the vault as `principal.mailbox.<id>.nylas_grant_id`.

    Repeat for each account. If you add more than one, click **Make default** on the one Curia should list first. Being the default doesn't decide where mail is sent from: Curia picks the account from the message, draft or meeting it's working on. You can rename a mailbox's label any time.

    Adding a mailbox also connects its calendar, so Curia can read and book on it straight away. Mailbox changes apply at once, with no restart.

    **If an account's access stops working** (a changed password, or access revoked in Google), click **Connect with Google** again for that account. It refreshes the existing mailbox rather than adding a second one. Curia's outage email to your principal includes a reconnect link that lasts 7 days, so they can do it from their phone. Pasting a new grant ID under **Edit** also still works.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn on the calendar specialist">
    The `calendar` agent is off on a new install. On the console's **Agents** page (open during Setup too), install and enable `calendar`.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Let your principal choose Email triage">
    Inbox triage is the **Email triage** capability: the `ceo-inbox` agent, the `ceo-inbox` tools, and the learning tools that let Curia learn from what your principal actually sends. It's off on a new install. Because it acts on your principal's own inbox, the Setup page leaves it for them to choose: its switch there is locked.

    After Setup, turn it on under **Settings → Behaviour → Capabilities → Email triage**, once your principal has agreed. See [Capabilities](/get-started/check-your-setup#capabilities).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Restart Curia">
    Curia reads agent and capability changes only when it starts. During Setup, use **Apply & restart**. After Setup, restart from the console's **Settings → System → About** page, or run:

    ```bash
    docker compose restart curia
    ```

    Adding or removing a mailbox doesn't need a restart.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **Upgrading from a single mailbox?** There's nothing to do. On its first start after the upgrade, Curia moves the existing principal mailbox into the list as the default, named after its address's domain (for example `gmail`). Rename it in the console if you like. Older vault keys for the mailbox and the Nylas key move to their new names at the same time. If email or the mailboxes then report the Nylas API key missing, save it again under **Settings → Channels → Email**.
</Note>

## Check it works

1. On **Settings → Behaviour → Email & Calendar**, each mailbox should be listed with the right address.
2. Open `/api/health` on your Curia address. `nylas_calendar` should read `ok`; it fails if any mailbox's calendars can't be reached. Once the inbox specialist is running, `principal_mailbox_triage` should read `ok` too.
3. Each mailbox also has its own daily check, named `principal_mailbox:<id>`. It runs with the daily canary and is logged as a `Health canary result` line with `"canary":"principal_mailbox:<id>"`. If a mailbox's access stops working, Curia alerts your principal once, with a reconnect link. Reconnect it from that link or with **Connect with Google**, or remove the mailbox if the account is gone.
4. In the console chat, ask "What's on my calendar tomorrow?" and check the answer against the real calendars.

Console chat counts as your principal, so keep test questions to reading, not changing.

## What your principal will notice

**In their inboxes**, every hour from 6am to 11pm by default:

- Curia works through unread mail. Messages it has dealt with are marked read or archived.
- Replies appear as **drafts** in the Drafts folder of the account the message arrived in, for them to review and send. Curia sends a draft when they ask, and can send routine replies itself if they allow it. See [Replies from your mailbox](/core-concepts/inbox-sending).
- Some messages get labels, such as `⏳ In Progress` while Curia waits on something, or `⚠️ Stuck` when it needs them.
- Anything urgent reaches them on another channel, such as Signal.
- A message sent to both of their addresses is handled once, not twice.

Your principal can give each inbox its own schedule just by asking, for example "check my work email every 30 minutes on weekdays, and my personal email once a day at 6pm". A schedule can run every so many minutes (in steps of 15) or hours, or once a day, within a time window and on weekdays only. A newly added mailbox starts with mail that arrives after it was connected, so an old backlog isn't worked through unless they ask ("go through the last week of my work email").

Behind the schedule, Curia looks every 15 minutes for a mailbox that's due and has unread mail, and only then starts work. Two limits keep a flood of mail in check:

- **Backlog alarm.** If a mailbox has more than 100 messages waiting, Curia tells your principal once.
- **Daily ceiling.** Curia triages at most about 600 messages a day across all mailboxes. If it reaches that, it tells your principal and stops until the next day.

If triage stops working, the `principal_mailbox_triage` check in `/api/health` reads `fail`.

**When Curia writes new mail or books a meeting**, it chooses the account from who it's for, the topic, past mail and your principal's standing preferences (such as "meetings always go on my work calendar"). It mentions the account only when the choice was a judgement call, and asks when it can't tell. A draft in the wrong account can be moved by asking ("move that to my personal account").

**On their calendars:**

- Curia answers scheduling questions from all of their calendars together. A meeting that's on two of their calendars is listed once. Your principal can tell Curia which events don't count as busy, for example "the cleaner's visits on my personal calendar don't block me".
- If a calendar doesn't answer, or has been removed, Curia never treats that time as free. It says which calendar it couldn't check.
- While it negotiates a time, it may place tentative events titled `HOLD (TBC): …`. It clears stale holds every night.
- It can accept invitations, answering from the account the invitation arrived in, and create events as them, including repeating ones such as a weekly one-on-one. At the default autonomy level (75), creating an event waits for their approval first.

**When your principal hands Curia a meeting to set up:**

- **Forwarding is enough.** When your principal forwards a request to Curia, Curia treats it as "please handle this" and tells them what it did, rather than asking first.
- **CC'd requests get times.** When your principal copies Curia on a thread to set up a meeting, Curia offers specific times from their calendar.
- **Booking links are used.** If the other person shared a booking page (Calendly and similar), Curia books through it instead of proposing times.
- **People they know are treated as known.** A first email from someone your principal has written to before (found in their Sent mail) is handled as a known contact, not a stranger, as long as the mail passes sender checks. You can also list your principal's company domains so colleagues count as known; see [Automatic elevation](/core-concepts/contacts-trust#automatic-elevation).
- **"Move things around."** When your principal asks Curia to make room, it moves their own blocks first, then small internal meetings, and never external or personal events. At an autonomy score of 80 or more it moves them and says what moved. Below 80 it asks first.

Changes appear as made by their own account, because Curia acts as them.

## Removing a mailbox

If your principal leaves a job or stops using an account, click **Remove** next to it in the console. The confirmation screen asks:

- **Is this address still yours?** Choose **No** if it now belongs to an old employer. Curia then stops treating mail from that address as your principal's.
- **A new primary email**, if the removed address was the one Curia uses to reach your principal. Curia won't keep sending its notices to an address they no longer read.
- **What to do with inbox rules** that applied only to that mailbox: delete them, or move them to another mailbox.

The confirmation screen also lists the calendars that go through that mailbox: removing it disconnects them. A removed mailbox's id is never reused, so nothing from the old account attaches to a new one, even one labelled the same. Its calendars are shown as removed rather than failing.

To switch jobs, add the new account, make it the default, then remove the old one.
