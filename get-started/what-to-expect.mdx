Curia works for one executive, the **principal**. Getting it running takes two phases, and they can be done by different people. This page covers who does each phase, how long it takes, what it costs, what you'll need, and how you'll know you're done.

## Two phases, two people

| | Setup | Onboarding |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | The **operator**: whoever installs and runs Curia. That might be an EA, IT, a technical friend, or the principal. | The **principal**: the executive Curia works for. |
| **What** | Install Curia, then connect its email, messaging, voice and Google access. | Get to know Curia: tell it how to work, then start using it. |
| **Where** | The installer, then the console's Setup page, with the setup guide chat beside it. | Conversations with Curia by message, email or voice. |

The Setup pages are written to the operator. They say whose mailbox or phone number each value belongs to: Curia's own, or the principal's.

**Setting Curia up for yourself?** Then you're both the operator and the principal. Do Setup first, then start using Curia as the principal. Where a page says "tell your principal", you can skip that step.

## How long it takes

Estimates as of 2026-11-25. They haven't yet been checked against a timed run-through, so treat them as rough.

- **Install: about 5 minutes.** One script, run on a machine with Docker.
- **Connect Curia: about 1–2 hours.** Work through it at your own pace. It can stretch over a few days if you're waiting on an account, a phone number or someone else's sign-off.
- **Onboarding: about half an hour of your principal's time** for the first conversation, then a review session once Curia has read their mailbox, and short check-ins after that.

## What you'll need

Gather these before you start. You don't need them all at once: each Setup page lists what it needs.

- **A machine with Docker and Docker Compose.** A small server works: 2 vCPU and 4 GB of memory plus swap. See [System requirements](/get-started/system-requirements).
- **A model provider account.** The installer asks which to use: Anthropic (an API key; the default), OpenRouter (an API key) or Amazon Bedrock (an AWS access key and region). With Bedrock, voice calls don't work yet. See [Model providers](/get-started/installation#model-providers).
- **A domain, or another HTTPS route to the server.** Telnyx needs a public URL to deliver texts, and browsers only allow the microphone for voice calls over HTTPS (or on the server itself). The installer offers a local option, but use it only to try Curia out.
- **A dedicated mailbox for Curia and a Nylas account.** Curia sends and receives email from its own address, through Nylas.
- **A way for Curia to message the principal.** The Curia app needs no number. Otherwise, a number registered with Signal (you run `signal-cli` yourself), a Telnyx number for text messages, or a Slack app in the principal's workspace.
- **Voice keys.** LiveKit comes bundled with Curia. You also need a Deepgram key (speech-to-text) and a Cartesia key (Curia's spoken voice).
- **Access to the principal's email and calendar.** The principal signs in with Google for each of their mailboxes (work and personal, say), through the same Nylas application. Google accounts only, for now.
- **A Google account for Curia**, plus a Google Cloud OAuth client, so Curia can create and share Google Docs. Curia delivers its first drafts to the principal this way, so this is required.
- **Optional: a Tavily key.** Web search works without one, but is capped at a few dozen searches an hour. A free key raises the limit.

## What it costs

Prices for the third-party services Curia uses, checked on the vendors' sites on 2026-11-01. They change, so check before you sign up.

| Service | What it's for | Price (checked 2026-11-01) |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic, OpenRouter or Amazon Bedrock | The language model | Pay as you go. Depends on how much Curia does, and on the models you choose. |
| Nylas | Curia's mailbox, and access to the principal's | Free: $0 for 5 accounts, described as "for proving it works" (production use isn't stated). Essentials: $15/month for 10 accounts. |
| Signal | Messaging the principal | Free |
| Telnyx | Messaging the principal by SMS (instead of Signal) | $0.004 per message part, plus carrier fees. Number and 10DLC registration fees vary. |
| Deepgram | Speech-to-text for voice calls | $200 free credit. Nova-3 streaming: $0.0077/minute. |
| Cartesia | Curia's spoken voice | Free: 20K credits (1 credit per character), which appears to lack a commercial licence. Pro: $5/month for 100K credits. |
| OpenAI embeddings (optional) | Curia's memory, only if you choose `text-embedding-3-small` over the bundled model | $0.02 per 1M tokens |
| Tavily | Web search. Works without a key; a key raises the limit. | Without a key: free, capped per hour. Free key: 1,000 credits/month, no card needed. |

LiveKit runs on your own server, so it adds no fee. Curia's memory runs on an embedding model bundled in the image, which adds no fee either.

## What your principal experiences

1. **Setup.** After install, the console opens on the Setup page. It asks who Curia is for: yourself, or someone else. If it's someone else, you add your own name and email as the operator, then the principal's details. The rest of Setup is about Curia's accounts and keys. Your principal sees none of it.
2. **Start onboarding.** When the required steps pass, **Start onboarding** ends Setup. Curia does some homework on your principal's public professional profile, then invites them: with an invitation link you send them for the Curia app, or a hello on Signal, text message or Slack.
3. **The first conversation.** About half an hour, by chat or voice, whenever suits them. Curia asks how they like to work: how it should sound, how independent it should be, when to interrupt them. It applies each choice as they make it. It also asks them to connect their email and calendar, and tells them exactly what it will do in their inbox before it does anything there. See [Your first conversation with Curia](/onboarding/first-conversation).
4. **The review.** Once their mailbox is connected, Curia reads about 90 days of it once and drafts three short documents: its working rules, where their time goes, and how they write. Nothing in them takes effect until your principal confirms it. See [Reviewing what Curia learned](/onboarding/reviewing-what-curia-learned).
5. **Check-ins, then a rhythm.** Curia books each next session with them, one at a time, then proposes a regular rhythm, such as a weekly session. See [Check-ins and your weekly session](/onboarding/check-ins-and-weekly-session).

From the first conversation on, the principal messages Curia in the Curia app, on Signal, by text message or on Slack, emails it, or talks to it. Voice calls happen in console chat or the phone app. See [Use Curia on your phone](/channels/phone-app).

The Onboarding tab has a page for each part, written to your principal. You can send them [Your first conversation with Curia](/onboarding/first-conversation) when you start onboarding.

## How you'll know you're done

Setup is done when every required step on the Setup page passes and you click **Start onboarding**. [The Setup page](/get-started/check-your-setup) also walks you through end-to-end checks, and explains how to hand Curia over to your principal.
