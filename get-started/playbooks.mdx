These playbooks are short, opinionated recipes for common Curia workflows. Each one stands on its own: pick the one that matches what you're trying to do.

## First run after install

You've installed Curia and signed in to the console with your bootstrap secret. Here's what happens next.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Work through the Setup page">
    A new install opens on the Setup page at `/setup`. It lists every step in order: who Curia is for, Curia's name, Email, Messaging, Voice, Email & calendar, Google Workspace, then Apply & restart. Each step takes its keys on the page and links to its guide. See [The Setup page](/get-started/check-your-setup).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Ask the setup guide when you're stuck">
    The setup guide, Curia's own chat, docks beside the Setup page. Click **Need help? Ask the setup guide** on any step to ask about it. It explains, compares options and checks what's missing; you fill in the steps.

    <Note>
      The setup guide checks progress against Curia's memory, which runs on the bundled embedding model out of the box. If memory is off, `/api/health` reports `knowledge_graph: "fail"`. See [Search and memory](/get-started/search-and-memory).
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Start onboarding">
    When the required steps pass, click **Start onboarding**. Setup ends, and Curia invites your principal to a first conversation: with an invitation link you send them for the Curia app, or a hello on Signal, text message or Slack. See [Start onboarding](/get-started/check-your-setup#start-onboarding) and, for your principal, [Your first conversation with Curia](/onboarding/first-conversation). After that, you can still ask Curia for help: "Help me set up email" reaches the setup guide through Curia's main chat.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  To revise Curia's name, signature or tone later, go to **Settings → Behaviour → Personality**. The Setup page closes after Start onboarding.
</Note>

## Calibrating autonomy

Curia ships with the autonomy engine seeded at **score 75 (approval-required band)**. At that level, Curia takes on work, coordinates and reports back on its own, but asks first before speaking or committing on the principal's behalf. Higher-risk tools — calendar writes and anything `critical` — also pause for explicit approval through the approval workflow.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Decide what you want to delegate">
    Read the [autonomy engine overview](/security/autonomy-engine) for the band model. Each band has a `min_score` floor that tools declare via `action_risk` in their manifests: `low` ≥ 60, `medium` ≥ 70, `high` ≥ 80, `critical` ≥ 90. Raising the score widens what runs without approval; lowering it tightens what slips through.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Adjust from the console">
    In the console, go to **Settings → Behaviour → Autonomy**. The page shows the current score and its recent changes, and lets you adjust the score with an optional reason. Every change is versioned to `autonomy_history`.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Watch a few cycles before raising further">
    The approval workflow surfaces a paused-action queue. After raising the score, review what Curia would have done autonomously over a few days. If nothing surprises you, you can raise again. If something feels too aggressive, lower it — there's no penalty for tightening.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Connecting another mailbox Curia owns

Curia can run more than one mailbox of its own, for example a second address for a different role. Each one is a separate Nylas grant on the same Nylas application. Add it in the console under **Settings → Channels → Email → Email accounts**, then restart Curia. See [Email](/channels/email-setup) for the details.

Your principal's own mailbox is different: Curia works in it as the principal rather than answering from it. Don't add it as an email account. See [Your principal's email and calendar](/get-started/principal-email-calendar).
