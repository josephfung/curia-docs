These playbooks are short, opinionated recipes for common Curia workflows. Each one stands on its own: pick the one that matches what you're trying to do.

## First run after setup

You've installed Curia, signed in to the console with your bootstrap secret, and filled in the `/setup` form. Here's what happens next.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Land in the chat view">
    Saving the last step of the form (**Confirm & save**) takes you to `/chat`. If email or Signal credentials were waiting for a restart, you'll see a brief **Setting up channels…** screen first while Curia restarts so those channels can come online. This usually takes 10–30 seconds.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Meet the setup assistant">
    A kickoff message fires automatically and the **setup-wizard** specialist introduces itself through the Coordinator. It starts with a few quick wins that need no credentials:

    - **Persona:** one open question about tone, priorities and how Curia should communicate.
    - **Debrief:** whether a regular digest would help, daily at the end of the day or weekly on Fridays. If yes, it schedules the job straight away. You can change or cancel it later from `/jobs` in the console, or by asking Curia.
    - **How to refer to the principal:** pronouns first, because emails go out in their name, then preferred name and title if the form didn't capture them.
    - **Capability tour:** the five or six things Curia can already do.

    Then it asks what you'd most like help with and starts that setup.

    <Note>
      The setup assistant checks progress against Curia's memory, so it needs an embedding source. Without one it can't tell what you've set up. See [Search and memory](/get-started/search-and-memory).
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect the rest">
    Work through the Setup pages in order: [Curia's own email](/get-started/curia-email), [Reach your principal](/get-started/reach-your-principal), [Voice](/get-started/voice), [Your principal's email and calendar](/get-started/principal-email-calendar), [Google Docs](/get-started/google-docs) and [Search and memory](/get-started/search-and-memory). Then confirm everything with [Check your setup](/get-started/check-your-setup).

    You can also ask the setup assistant for help at any time: "Help me set up email" or "What can you do?" both route back to it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  To revise the assistant's identity or tone later, visit `/setup` directly. The form stays available after setup and pre-fills with the current values.
</Note>

## Calibrating autonomy

Curia ships with the autonomy engine seeded at **score 75 (approval-required band)**. At that level, reads and internal state writes (memory, contacts, behavioral preferences) and outbound communications (email and Signal sends) run autonomously, while higher-risk actions — calendar writes, commitments on the CEO's behalf, and anything `critical` — pause for explicit approval through the approval workflow.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Decide what you want to delegate">
    Read the [autonomy engine overview](/security/autonomy-engine) for the band model. Each band has a `min_score` floor that tools declare via `action_risk` in their manifests: `low` ≥ 60, `medium` ≥ 70, `high` ≥ 80, `critical` ≥ 90. Raising the score widens what runs without approval; lowering it tightens what slips through.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Adjust from the console">
    In the console, go to `/settings/autonomy`. The page shows the current score, lets you preview the consequences of a change on a slider, and asks for a written reason on save. Every change is versioned to `autonomy_history`.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Watch a few cycles before raising further">
    The approval workflow surfaces a paused-action queue. After raising the score, review what Curia would have done autonomously over a few days. If nothing surprises you, you can raise again. If something feels too aggressive, lower it — there's no penalty for tightening.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Connecting another mailbox Curia owns

Curia can run more than one mailbox of its own, for example a second address for a different role. Each one is a separate Nylas grant on the same Nylas application. Add it in the console under **Settings → Channels → Email → Email accounts**, then restart Curia. See [Email](/channels/email-setup) for the details.

Your principal's own mailbox is different: Curia works in it as the principal rather than answering from it. Don't add it as an email account. See [Your principal's email and calendar](/get-started/principal-email-calendar).
