Curia can work in your email and calendar the way an executive assistant with delegated access would. This page explains what you're agreeing to and what you'll notice. It takes about two minutes to read, and the sign-in itself takes less.

## What "delegated access" means

You sign in to your Google account once and approve access for Curia. From then on Curia can read your mail, draft replies and read and update your calendar, as you, without your password. It never sees your password. You can take the access away at any time in your Google account's security settings, or by asking whoever set Curia up to remove the mailbox.

Only Google accounts (Gmail and Google Workspace) can be connected today.

## What Curia will and won't do

**It will:**

- Read your unread mail and sort it. It marks what it has dealt with as read, puts some messages under labels, and archives routine ones.
- Write draft replies for you and leave them for you to review.
- Check your calendar, and set up meetings and holds when you ask it to or hand it a request.
- Tell you on another channel, such as Signal, when something is urgent.

**It won't:**

- Send a reply for you without your say-so, unless you've told it routine replies are fine. By default, drafts wait for you.
- Put a meeting on your calendar without asking first, at the default setting.
- Delete your mail. Archiving moves a message out of the inbox, and nothing more.
- Touch accounts you haven't connected.

## What to expect

**Labels and archiving.** Mail Curia has dealt with leaves your inbox and is archived. Archived mail is not deleted: it's in **All Mail** in Gmail, and search finds it. Some messages get a label such as `⏳ In Progress` while Curia waits on something, or `⚠️ Stuck` when it needs you.

**Drafts.** Replies appear in your Drafts folder, in the account the message arrived in. They sit there **unsent** until you send them or ask Curia to. It's normal to see several drafts waiting. It means Curia is working, not that something broke.

**Your calendar.** When Curia is working out a time, it may put a tentative event on your calendar titled `HOLD (TBC): …` and clears stale ones every night. Curia may also put sessions with you, such as a review of its drafts, on your calendar as real events. Whether Curia **asks you before it books a meeting** follows the independence level you pick in your first conversation with it. At the default level, it asks first.

Curia will walk you through all of this in your first conversation.

## When you sign in

You'll get a link, from Curia or from whoever set it up. It works once and expires after an hour. If it runs out, ask Curia for a new one.

1. **Open the link and choose your Google account.** Choose the account the link was meant for.
2. **Google shows a screen saying the app isn't verified.** This is expected. Curia's connection isn't published in Google's directory, because it's set up just for you, so Google warns about every app like it. Open **Advanced** and choose to continue to the app. If you're signed in to a Google Workspace account at work and Google says an admin has blocked this, ask your IT admin to mark Curia's app as **Trusted**.
3. **Google asks for access to your mail and calendar. Keep every box ticked.** Google may show this once, or on two separate screens. If you untick one, the sign-in ends with a "Permissions missing" message and nothing is connected. Open the link again and keep them all ticked.
4. **You'll see "Connected".** You can close the window.

If you get an error page instead, it says what to do. The common ones are "link expired" (ask for a new one) and "wrong account" (open the link again and choose the right account).

## If you change your Google password

A password change makes Google cancel the access Curia holds for that account. Curia notices, and sends you an email with a link to sign in again. The link lasts a week. Open it and follow the same steps. Nothing else is lost.
