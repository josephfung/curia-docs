Curia is an assistant that works for you: your email, your calendar, research and follow-ups. Before it does anything on your behalf, it wants to learn how you like to work. That's your first conversation. It takes about half an hour, by chat or by voice, and you can stop and pick it up later.

This page takes about three minutes to read.

## How Curia gets in touch

Whoever set Curia up for you has started your onboarding. You'll hear from Curia in one of two ways:

- **An invitation link,** sent to you by whoever set Curia up. Open it on your phone in Safari or Chrome. It installs the Curia app and signs you in, and Curia says hello once you're in. The link works once and lasts 48 hours. If it runs out, ask for a new one.
- **A hello from Curia** on Signal, by text message or on Slack. On Signal, the first message comes from a number you don't know, so Signal shows it as a message request. Accept it.

The hello is short: who Curia is, who set it up, and an offer of a first conversation. Reply whenever suits you. If now isn't good, say when is ("Thursday at 4?") and Curia books it.

If you don't reply, Curia follows up twice, two days apart, then lets whoever set it up know.

## Chat or voice

Your choice. Type your answers wherever Curia said hello, or talk:

- **In the Curia app** or in the console, tap **Voice** and allow the microphone.
- **On Signal**, call Curia's number, if your setup includes Signal calls.

You can switch between them. What you say on a call is applied the same way as in chat.

## What Curia asks

Mostly multiple choice, one topic at a time, in whatever order suits the conversation. After each one, Curia reads back what it heard and puts it into effect straight away. You can skip anything.

- **About you.** Curia did a little homework beforehand: your company, role and public bio, and recent news. Nothing about your family, home or personal accounts. It tells you what it found and asks what it got wrong. It remembers only what you confirm. Then a few open questions: the people who matter most, what a good week looks like.
- **How it should sound.** Brief or thorough, dry or a bit of humour, warm or businesslike. Curia shows you two lines in the style you pick, and you can tweak it once.
- **How independent it should be.**
  - **Draft only:** Curia prepares everything; you send and decide.
  - **Ask first** (recommended): Curia handles routine things and checks with you before anything significant.
  - **Spot-check:** Curia acts on most things and you review afterwards.
- **How to reach you.** What's urgent enough to interrupt you, which channel for urgent things and which for routine ones, quiet hours, and whether you'd like a daily recap.
- **Your email and calendar.** Curia sends you a sign-in link and explains what access means first. See [Connecting your email and calendar](/onboarding/connecting-your-email).
- **What you'll notice in your inbox.** Before Curia touches your mail, it tells you exactly what it will do there, and asks your choices: labels on or off, whether it may send anything itself or only draft, and whether mail it has dealt with should leave your inbox. Curia does nothing in your inbox until this part is done.
- **Where your files live,** and which of them Curia should read or leave alone.
- **How you'd like to review its work:** together on a call, or on your own and then a message.
- **A first look.** With your mailbox connected, Curia offers a few concrete things, such as drafting replies to the three most pressing messages, or clearing the obvious noise. Each one is up to you.
- **What happens next.** Curia agrees a time for the next session and books it.

Ask Curia for anything else along the way. It treats a side request as its own piece of work, not something to save for later.

## If you stop part-way

Nothing is lost. Curia remembers which topics are done. If you go quiet for a few hours mid-conversation, it nudges you once, then picks up the rest next time. If you agreed a later time, it waits until then.

To change something you chose, just tell Curia ("be a bit less formal"). To stop the onboarding altogether, say "stop the onboarding". Curia then leaves your inbox alone until you switch it on.
