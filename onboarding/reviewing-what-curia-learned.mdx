Once your email and calendar are connected, Curia looks back over about the last 90 days once, so it doesn't have to learn everything from scratch. It turns what it sees into three short drafts for you to check. **Nothing in them takes effect until you confirm it.**

## What Curia reads

Curia reads the outline of your mail and calendar: who you write to and hear from, subjects, folders, and when your meetings happen. To learn how you write, it also reads a small sample of messages you sent. It does this once, and works from its summary afterwards, not from your mailbox.

It also adds the people you correspond with most to your contacts (up to 150): people you've exchanged at least three messages with, at least one each way, and regulars at meetings you organised or accepted. Newsletters and automated senders are left out.

On a large mailbox, Curia stops at a limit and says so in the drafts, so you know what it didn't see.

## The three drafts

Each draft opens with "nothing here is in effect yet".

- **Working rules.** How you seem to handle your mail and calendar, written as rules Curia could follow: who gets a quick answer, what can wait, what never goes on your calendar.
- **Time and priorities.** Where your time went over those months, and what that suggests you're trying to move.
- **Writing voice.** How you write: tone, length, how you open and sign off. Curia uses it when it drafts for you.

If something in a draft contradicts what you told Curia in your first conversation, the draft points it out. What you said wins.

## How the review works

When the drafts are ready, Curia shares each one with you as a Google Doc you can edit, tells you on your usual channel, and books the review the way you chose in your first conversation: together on a call, or on your own and then a message.

For each document you can:

- **Confirm it** as written.
- **Correct it,** by editing the Doc or by telling Curia what to change.
- **Set it aside.** Nothing from it is used.

Before Curia takes on any working rules, it reads back a one-line list of the rules it's about to add and waits for your yes. Your confirmation has to come from you in the conversation: a note in the Doc or an email isn't enough.

Once you've confirmed a document, Curia moves it into its own records, adds a line to the top of the Doc saying where it now lives, and sends you a short receipt.

## Later

To have Curia look again later ("go through my mailbox again"), just ask. It starts fresh and drafts the documents again for you to review.
