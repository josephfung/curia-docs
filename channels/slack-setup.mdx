Slack lets your team reach your Curia agent the same way they'd reach a colleague: DM it, @mention it in a channel, or keep talking in a thread after the first mention. Some principals prefer it to Signal as their everyday channel.

Slack does not allow software to join as a normal human user. Instead you create a **workspace-owned Slack app** (a bot user), install it, and store its tokens in Curia's vault. Day-to-day the experience is ordinary Slack — invite the bot to channels, DM it, @mention it.

<Info>
  Trust rides the **sender**, not the channel. Slack is a `medium`-trust channel *floor*, but once your Slack user id is linked to your principal contact, your Slack messages carry principal-tier trust — the same unified contact ledger as email and Signal. For high-autonomy approvals when your Slack identity isn't linked yet, prefer Signal or the CLI.
</Info>

## Prerequisites

- A Slack workspace where you (or an admin) can create apps
- Your Curia **office identity** name (e.g. "Nathan Curia") — you'll use this as the Slack bot's display name and @handle. Curia never assumes `@curia`; the handle comes from your Slack app settings.

## Step 1 — Create the Slack app from Curia's manifest

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the app from a manifest">
    Open [api.slack.com/apps](https://api.slack.com/apps) → **Create New App** → **From a manifest**, choose your workspace, and paste the manifest below.

    ```yaml
    display_information:
      name: YOUR_OFFICE_IDENTITY_NAME
      description: Curia agent for this workspace — DMs, @mentions, and in-thread replies.
      background_color: "#1A6B5E"

    features:
      bot_user:
        display_name: YOUR_OFFICE_IDENTITY_NAME
        always_online: true
      app_home:
        home_tab_enabled: false
        messages_tab_enabled: true
        messages_tab_read_only_enabled: false

    oauth_config:
      scopes:
        bot:
          - app_mentions:read
          - chat:write
          - im:history
          - im:read
          - im:write
          - channels:history
          - groups:history
          - users:read
          - reactions:read

    settings:
      event_subscriptions:
        bot_events:
          - app_mention
          - message.im
          - message.channels
          - message.groups
          - reaction_added
      interactivity:
        is_enabled: false
      org_deploy_enabled: false
      socket_mode_enabled: true
      token_rotation_enabled: false
    ```
  </Step>

  <Step title="Name the bot after your office identity">
    Replace `YOUR_OFFICE_IDENTITY_NAME` (in both `display_information.name` and `bot_user.display_name`) with your office identity, e.g. `Nathan Curia`. If Slack prompts for a bot username, pick something like `nathancuria` or `nathan`.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create the app">
    Review the summary and create the app.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 2 — Enable Socket Mode and get the tokens

Curia connects over Socket Mode, so there's no public webhook URL to host.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enable Socket Mode">
    In the app settings, open **Socket Mode** and toggle it on.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create an App-Level Token">
    Create an **App-Level Token** with the `connections:write` scope. Copy the `xapp-…` token — this is your **App-Level Token**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Install and copy the Bot Token">
    Open **OAuth & Permissions** → **Install to Workspace** (or reinstall after any manifest scope/event change). Copy the **Bot User OAuth Token** (`xoxb-…`).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 3 — Store the tokens in the vault

In the Curia console, go to **Settings → Channels → Slack** and enter both tokens. They're stored in the [vault](/get-started/secrets-vault):

| Field | Vault key |
|---|---|
| Bot User OAuth Token (`xoxb-…`) | `channel.slack.bot_token` |
| App-Level Token (`xapp-…`) | `channel.slack.app_token` |

Then install and enable the Slack channel in the registry and restart Curia so the adapter starts:

```bash
docker compose restart curia
```

<Note>
  `restart` re-runs Curia's startup so it re-reads the vault (and registry) — that's all this step needs, and it leaves the container and its volumes untouched. If instead you set the tokens via environment variables in `.env`, use `docker compose up -d curia` — a plain restart won't pick up changed env vars.
</Note>

## Step 4 — Send your first message

<Steps>
  <Step title="Invite the bot or open a DM">
    Invite the bot to a channel, or open a direct message with it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="DM or @mention">
    Send a DM, or @mention the bot in a channel. Curia replies in-thread for channel mentions.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Continue in the thread">
    After the first @mention you can keep talking in that thread without re-mentioning — Curia stays active in the thread until the process restarts.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Link your Slack identity to the principal">
    Ask Curia to link your Slack user id to your principal contact so your Principal Contact Details include Slack and principal-tier trust applies on Slack turns:

    ```
    > My Slack user id is U0123456789 — that's me (the principal).
    ```
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Unknown workspace members who message the bot are auto-created as `tier=unknown` contacts — the same low-trust constraints as an unknown emailer — until you elevate them or link their Slack identity onto an existing contact.
</Note>

### Optional: restrict to specific channels

To limit @mentions and in-thread channel replies to specific Slack channels, set an allowlist in `config/local.yaml`:

```yaml
channels:
  slack:
    allowed_channel_ids:
      - C0123456789
```

DMs are never filtered by this list. An empty or absent list means all channels the bot is a member of.

## Reactions

Curia normalizes Slack reactions into a channel-agnostic reaction signal (a reactor's 👍 on a message). Emoji-to-intent mapping — for example reading 👍 on an approval request as approval — is a follow-up; today reactions are captured but not yet acted on.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Adapter not starting">
    Confirm that:

    1. Both tokens are in the vault (**Settings → Channels → Slack**)
    2. Slack is installed **and** enabled in **Settings → Channels**
    3. Curia was restarted after enabling (`docker compose restart curia`)
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="No replies in a channel">
    Check that the bot is invited to the channel, that the first turn is an @mention (or that you're continuing an already-active thread), and — if you configured an allowlist — that the channel id is on it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Thread replies ignored after a restart">
    Active-thread state is process-local and is lost on restart. @mention the bot once to re-activate the thread.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Socket Mode keeps disconnecting">
    Make sure the App-Level Token has the `connections:write` scope and that the bot token is from the **same** app. Check `docker compose logs curia` for details.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Curia treats me as an unknown sender">
    Link your Slack user id to your principal contact (Step 4). Elevate colleagues' tiers as needed.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Missing reactions or channel messages">
    Re-install the app after updating the manifest — the newer events and scopes (`reaction_added`, `message.channels`, `message.groups`, `reactions:read`) only take effect on reinstall.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
