SMS lets Curia text people the way an assistant with an office line would: send a message to your principal or a third party, and field replies on the same number. It rides a **dedicated Telnyx number** (a DID) that belongs to Curia's office, not a ported personal or consumer-app number.

Two phone numbers are involved. Keep them apart:

- **The office number.** Curia's own Telnyx number. Curia texts from it, and your principal texts it.
- **Your principal's mobile.** Their own phone. You link it to your principal's contact in Curia, so Curia knows who's texting.

<Note>
  Choosing between SMS and Signal? See [3. Reach your principal](/get-started/reach-your-principal).
</Note>

Inbound texts arrive as a signed webhook from Telnyx; outbound sends go through Curia's outbound gateway to the Telnyx Messages API. Conversations are 1:1 text only in v1 (no MMS or group messaging).

<Info>
  Trust rides the **sender**, not the channel. SMS is a `medium`-trust channel *floor* — carrier From headers are spoofable and SMS is not end-to-end encrypted, so it must not match Signal's `high`. Once your principal's mobile is linked to their contact as a verified `sms` identity, their texts carry principal-tier trust. For approvals that need higher assurance, Signal or the console is the better channel.
</Info>

## Prerequisites

- A **public HTTPS URL** for your Curia host — Telnyx must be able to reach the webhook (unlike Signal's local socket).
- A [Telnyx Mission Control](https://telnyx.com) account with a funded balance.
- For a **US** long code: a brand plus a **Low-Volume Mixed** 10DLC campaign (Sole Proprietor registration is fine if you have no EIN). A **Canadian** office DID is a viable alternative that sits outside 10DLC — different regulatory and cost trade-offs, no campaign registration.

<Note>
  This is Curia's SMS **transport** — a new office number. Do not port a personal or consumer-app number (TextNow and the like): there's no suitable server API for cold inbound plus authenticated send, and it muddies the office identity.
</Note>

## Step 1 — Buy a number and point its webhook at Curia

<Steps>
  <Step title="Complete 10DLC (US long code only)">
    In Telnyx Mission Control, register your brand and a **Low-Volume Mixed** 10DLC campaign. Skip this step for a Canadian DID.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Buy a voice+SMS number">
    Purchase a **voice+SMS** capable number. Prefer voice+SMS so the same DID can be reused if you later add a phone/voice stack.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Attach a Messaging Profile and set the webhook">
    Create or attach a Messaging Profile and set its webhook URL (API v2) to:

    ```
    https://<your-host>/api/webhooks/telnyx/sms
    ```

    Curia verifies every webhook's Ed25519 signature and rejects unsigned or invalid requests, so this endpoint is safe to expose publicly.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Copy the three credentials">
    From Mission Control, copy your **API key**, your **office DID** in E.164 (e.g. `+14155550123`), and your account **Public Key** (Keys & Credentials) used to verify webhook signatures.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 2 — Store the credentials in the vault

In the Curia console, go to **Settings → Channels** and click the **sms** row. Click **Install** if it isn't installed yet, then save the three values under **Credentials**. They're stored in the [vault](/get-started/secrets-vault):

| Field | Vault key |
|---|---|
| Telnyx API key | `channel.sms.api_key` |
| Office DID (E.164): Curia's office number | `channel.sms.from_number` |
| Telnyx webhook public key | `channel.sms.webhook_public_key` |

<Note>
  SMS credentials are **vault-only** — there is no environment-variable fallback. Set all three in the console before enabling the channel.
</Note>

Then click **Enable** and restart Curia, from the folder where you ran the installer, so the adapter starts and installs the webhook handler:

```bash
docker compose restart curia
```

<Note>
  `restart` re-runs Curia's startup so it re-reads the vault and registry — it leaves the container and its volumes untouched. Until the adapter is started, the webhook endpoint returns `503` so Telnyx retries after you enable and restart.
</Note>

## Step 3 — Link your principal's mobile

This is the step people miss. Without it, Curia treats texts from your principal as coming from a stranger, and won't recognize texts it sends to your principal as going to them.

1. In the console, open **Contacts** and select your principal.
2. Under **Channel identities**, choose the channel **sms**.
3. Enter **your principal's** mobile number in E.164 format as the **Identifier**.
4. Click **Add identity**.

Numbers you add here are saved as verified and take effect straight away, with no restart.

The `sms` identity is separate from the contact's `phone` channel: `phone` is a number on file, while `sms` is the number Curia texts and recognizes. Never enter the **office number** here.

## Check it works

- **Health.** `curl https://<your-domain>/api/health` shows `"sms": "ok"` once the adapter has started and installed its webhook handler. `"skipped"` means the channel didn't start.
- **Console.** The sms row in **Settings → Channels** shows **enabled**, and your principal's contact lists a verified **sms** identity.
- **A real text.** Text the office number from your own phone. Curia replies on the same number. If you aren't the principal, Curia treats you as an unknown sender and answers with care, and that's expected. SMS has no threads, so Curia uses [context bridging](/channels/how-channels-work#context-bridging-on-non-threaded-channels) to keep track of the conversation.

## What your principal will notice

Nothing until Curia texts them. Curia's texts come from the office number. Give your principal that number to save as a contact, so they know who's texting.

<Note>
  Unknown senders who text the office number are auto-created as `tier=unknown` contacts, and their `sms` identity is **not** auto-verified (carrier From is spoofable) — the same low-trust constraints as an unknown emailer until you elevate or link them.
</Note>

## Opt-out (STOP)

Telnyx enforces carrier opt-out at the platform level: once someone texts `STOP` (or `CANCEL`, `END`, `QUIT`, `UNSUBSCRIBE`), sends to that number are rejected with error **40300**. Curia does **not** keep a parallel suppression list.

Inbound `STOP` — or a natural-language "please stop texting me" — reaches the agent as an ordinary message. Curia handles it conversationally and records the preference as a fact on the contact, and it treats a 40300 bounce as authoritative rather than retrying. There are no app-level keyword auto-replies.

## Cost

Checked 2026-11-01: Telnyx charges $0.004 per SMS message part, plus carrier fees. The number's monthly rent and any 10DLC registration fees vary by country and number type; check Telnyx's pricing for yours.

## Non-goals (v1)

- Porting a consumer number (TextNow, etc.) as the office DID
- MMS and group messaging
- In-app Telnyx OAuth or automated number purchase

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Adapter not starting">
    Confirm that:

    1. All three credentials are in the vault (**Settings → Channels → SMS**)
    2. SMS is installed **and** enabled in **Settings → Channels**
    3. Curia was restarted after enabling (`docker compose restart curia`)
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Webhook returns 503">
    The route is mounted but the adapter hasn't started — the channel isn't enabled yet, or Curia hasn't been restarted since you enabled it. Telnyx retries, so a text sent during setup lands once the adapter is up.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Inbound texts rejected (401)">
    The webhook signature failed to verify. Check that `channel.sms.webhook_public_key` is the account **Public Key** from Mission Control (Keys & Credentials), copied exactly, and that the Messaging Profile webhook points at `/api/webhooks/telnyx/sms`.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Outbound send blocked as opted out">
    A 40300 from Telnyx means that number texted `STOP` and is suppressed at the carrier. This is authoritative — the recipient must text `START` to resume before Curia can message them again.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Curia treats my principal as an unknown sender">
    Link your principal's mobile to their contact as an `sms` identity (Step 3). A `phone` identity alone doesn't grant principal-tier trust on SMS.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
